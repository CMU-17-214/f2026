# Labs

We have weekly labs, due each Friday **by the end of your recitation section**. Lab handouts release several days before the lab sessions each week. Labs apply the week's lecture to a concrete artifact (a small service, a codebase to critique, a pipeline to stand up) or build skills you need for an upcoming assignment. 

Each lab has **three concrete milestones**, and is designed to take under an hour of work (though it can take longer if you run into technical difficulties or are not comfortable with lecture concepts). You earn the lab's credit by showing all three milestones to a TA at recitation and explaining what happened and why. Think of it as interview practice. You will be making design decisions and defending them for technical interviews, and this is just a low-stakes setting to practice that. Attendance for recitations is required to get credit for a lab. You should attempt the lab *before* the recitation session, since you may not be able to finish in time for a TA to give you feedback otherwise. (This probably doesn't need to be said, but your agent is not invited for the interaction with the TA. Recitation questions are answered by *you*. :-))

Labs are deliberately lower stakes than assignments. They are graded for completeness, not correctness, and mistakes are a normal (and expected) part of the process. If your solution or explanation does not meet the requirements, you can continue to work on it during the lab session until it does (or you run out of time, whichever comes first). 

We drop the two lowest grades you get for labs. 

## Collaboration policy

You can discuss labs with other students and help each other get unstuck, both before and during recitation. Your milestones and your explanations to the TA must be your own. Copying another student's work or presenting work you cannot explain defeats the purpose and does not earn any credit for the lab.
